Detailed Description

Drives a hobby RC servo, with the official Servo library's interface: attach()/detach(), write() in degrees or microseconds, writeMicroseconds(), read() and readMicroseconds().

On a pin with PWM – PWM0-PWM5 on every board, and also D3, D5, D6 and D9 on FRDM-MCXA156 and FRDM-MCXN236 (on FRDM-MCXN236 these are four of PWM0-PWM5), and on FRDM-MCXC444 D3, D5, D6, D9 and D10, as on an UNO – the pulses come from the PWM hardware, at 50Hz, so their width doesn't jitter.

On any other digital pin, a timer interrupt raises and lowers the pin, as the official library does on AVR: one pulse after another, every 20ms, or longer when the pulses add up to more (12 servos at 2400us take about 29ms). Other interrupts can make these pulses a few microseconds longer or shorter, as on AVR. The timer is CTIMER2 (CTIMER3 on FRDM-MCXN947, where MCXEthernet uses CTIMER2), and TPM2 on FRDM-MCXC444. It runs only while such a servo is attached. A pulse is at most 20000us here.

The two pins of a FlexPWM submodule share their period (see the board's PIN_MAPPING_*.md): attaching a servo sets the other pin's PWM to 50Hz too, and analogWriteFrequency() on the other pin changes the servo's. Another servo, or analogWrite() at 50Hz, on the other pin is fine. On FRDM-MCXC444 the period is shared by a whole TPM: D5, D6, D9 and D10 (TPM0), and D3 (TPM1). D5, D6 and D9 share one TPM0 channel as well, so only one servo among them runs at a time: the one written last.

Unlike the bundled mcxRCServo library, this leaves analogWrite()'s resolution alone.
